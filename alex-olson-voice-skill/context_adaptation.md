# Context Adaptation

## Emails

Voice: polite, direct, practical, low-drama.

Recommended structure:

1. Brief greeting.
2. One-sentence context.
3. What Alex is doing or asking.
4. Relevant constraints, options, or uncertainty.
5. Clear next step.

Keep the tone friendly, but do not make it sound like marketing copy. Avoid excessive gratitude or enthusiasm.

## Technical explanations

Voice: clear, mechanism-first, practical.

Recommended structure:

1. What it is.
2. Why it matters.
3. How it works.
4. Where it fails.
5. What to do about it.

Define the system before evaluating it. Connect technical architecture to human consequences: efficiency, transparency, access, security, scalability, reliability, or maintainability.

## Essays

Voice: explanatory, earnest, and source-aware.

Recommended structure:

1. Establish historical, technical, or social context.
2. Define the system or conflict.
3. Explain why the conflict matters.
4. Give concrete examples.
5. End with broader implications.

Do not make the essay too slick. Preserve causal reasoning and intellectual curiosity, while cleaning up long paragraphs.

## Personal reflections

Voice: candid, grounded, self-critical, not sentimental.

Useful pattern:

1. I used to think or do X.
2. A specific event, difficulty, or practice forced Y.
3. The process was harder than expected.
4. Here is what changed.
5. Here is what I still have to work on.

Personal writing should feel honest and practical. Avoid melodrama.

## Persuasive or public-facing writing

Voice: morally aware, concrete, values-driven, but still mechanism-based.

Recommended structure:

1. Start with a concrete problem, question, or lived consequence.
2. Explain the system that creates the problem.
3. Show the harm or inefficiency.
4. Address the obvious counterargument.
5. Propose a better path.
6. End with agency, repair, justice, or community.

This context can use stronger rhetorical questions and sharper contrasts.

## SOPs, runbooks, and documentation

Voice: utilitarian, concise, and exact. Personality should mostly disappear.

Recommended sections:

1. Purpose.
2. Prerequisites.
3. Steps.
4. Known failure modes.
5. Verification.
6. Follow-up or cleanup.

Use numbered steps, substeps, imperative verbs, and clear verification points.

## Critiques and reviews

Voice: fair, specific, direct, and constructive.

Recommended structure:

1. What worked.
2. What did not work.
3. Why it matters.
4. What would improve it.
5. Overall judgment.

Avoid needless harshness. Criticism should be specific enough to be useful.
